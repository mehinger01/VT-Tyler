1. Course Description

ECON 200 introduces economic models used to analyze the behavior of individuals and firms and the market outcomes that result from their decisions. The course examines incentives, buyer and seller interaction, prices, firm production decisions, market structures, and government intervention. Students are expected not only to define concepts but also to apply economic models to unfamiliar situations.

2. Prerequisites

Students must have completed the required introductory mathematics prerequisite or have appropriate university mathematics placement. ECON 200 should normally be completed before Principles of Macroeconomics.

3. Learning Outcomes

Students should be able to use microeconomic terminology; explain scarcity, opportunity cost, incentives, and marginal decision-making; construct and interpret supply-and-demand models; distinguish movement along a curve from a shift; predict equilibrium changes; interpret elasticity; analyze consumer and producer decisions; calculate costs and profit; explain market structures; analyze market failures; evaluate government intervention; and apply economic reasoning to unfamiliar problems.

4. Required Materials

Textbook: Principles of Microeconomics, digital edition. The digital course package includes Achieve for required reading checks and problem sets. Students should obtain access during the first week.
